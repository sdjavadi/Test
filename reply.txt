I checked this on our edge tables (Jun–Aug 2026, staging data). Short answer: name = own FI is mostly real activity, not a data quality issue.

Size: ~67K of 78.8M counterparty ids (0.09%), $419B of $8.4T.
What they are:
• Customers paying the bank itself, i.e. card / loan payments: Capital One (~1.9M PNC customer links), Bank of America (327K customers), Chase (180K customers, ~$444 per payment)
• Interbank funding / correspondent wires: AgriBank, CoBank fed funds, FHLB Pittsburgh, BNY Mellon ($5M–$190M per txn)
• The bank paying its own customers (39K ids, small tickets)

The misleading links from name matching are the opposite case: the name is a bank, but the account sits at a different bank. Examples: RABOBANK NEDERLAND at JPMorgan ($3.4B), TD / RBC / Scotiabank at BofA or JPM, credit unions at their corporate CUs, PNC BANK at Wells / State Street. Routing-based mapping links these to the bank that holds the account, which is correct. So the key-based fix is right, but please don't drop the same-name links.

Real data quality issues we found:
1. The FI name for routing 124003116 is spelled "ALLLY BANK" (three Ls) on all 160K ids. The routing itself is right: the other ids under it are Ally depositors, and the 77 "ALLY BANK" counterparties would match their own FI exactly if the name were spelled correctly. So this is a reference-name typo, not a mapping error.
2. 232 routing numbers map to 2–3 FI names (1.2% of ids). Most look like acquisitions (Comerica / Fifth Third, Cadence / Huntington, FirstBank / PNC) or spelling variants (GECU vs GECU FEDERAL CREDIT UNION). Key-based mapping needs one canonical name per routing number, otherwise one routing number creates two CptyFinEntity nodes.
3. Aliases such as M&T BANK vs MANUFACTURERS AND TRADERS TRUST COMPANY.
