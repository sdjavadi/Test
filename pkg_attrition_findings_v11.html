This report answers the two questions an RM asks when a name lands on their list. Why is this client on my list? covers which signals carry the attrition score and which earn nothing. Question 1

What drives the attrition score

The refit is stable: seven rolling test months, every fit converged, top-decile calibration 1.011. Each signal was measured three ways: alone, by the AUC lost when it is removed and the model refitted, and by the AUC lost when it is shuffled.

AUC and dollars disagree about which families matter

AUC averages the whole book; the queue only ever reaches the top 1,000 a month. Judged on the dollars the queue reaches, the ranking of families changes.

cptya_out earns nothing. Removing it costs 0.0000 AUC and $6.5m (not distinguishable from zero), yet it is the top reason on 20.9% of alerts, of which only 2.2% left. A spec without it is the next thing to test.

The model cannot be cut to 10 signals. The top 10 fall 0.016 AUC and $85.2m [$20.5m–$159.6m] short; the top 40 come within 0.003 AUC and $17.9m (interval crosses zero). About 40 signals is the floor.

Never show RMs coefficients. 25 of 74 signals have a model sign opposite to their standalone direction. Reason codes explain the ranking, not the client's motive.

Question 2

Why they leave: moving the banking, or shrinking the business

Three kinds of payment evidence separate the stories. Money sent to the client's own name at another bank points to a move. Former PNC trading partners still trading with the client elsewhere after exit points to a move. Those partners going quiet points to a shrinking business. Every threshold is calibrated so it fires for at most 5% of comparable clients who stayed.

Why the old 88% headline was wrong

Earlier versions reported that 88% of typed departing dollars were displacement. That compared a detector that sees nearly everyone with one that sees a fifth of clients, so it leaned toward displacement by construction. The honest comparison is made only where both answers were possible.

Seeing the type while the money is still here

The direct evidence mostly appears around and after exit, which is too late for an RM. So the directly typed eligible clients (717) became labels for a model that predicts the type from what is visible at the alert.

From finding to action

The plays

COMPETE

Lead with pricing, ECR and service. The client looks like it is moving its banking.

SUPPORT

Lead with credit, working capital or restructuring. Only issued where contraction is observable.

DISCOVER

Ask before offering. The evidence does not point either way.

VERIFY_INTERNAL

Check for a re-key or a move to a sibling entity first.

Closing the range

What will settle the answer

The 30–66% range comes from two groups of eligible attriters: 714 called CONTRACTION, some of which may be moves the name matcher missed, and 296 whose partners are still alive but where no move was seen. RMs are reviewing 40 of each. Their verdicts turn the range into one number.

Before this reaches an RM

Caveats and next steps

  • The typing is a hypothesis about why, built from payment behaviour. It becomes a finding when RMs confirm the review sample, and a product when win/loss outcomes are recorded against the play they were given.
  • A play is a conversation opener, not an offer. Routing credit offers by this score needs a fair-lending review first; destination data needs the permissible-use read.
  • No institution or client is named here. Full identifiers stay in the notebook's access-controlled CSVs.
  • Small cells are small. The $10m+ band has 29 eligible attriters; the play replication rests on 28 and 20 departing clients.
