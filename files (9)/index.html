The shape of the work

Eight runs over roughly a year. Three produced results that were later withdrawn, and the reasons are more instructive than the numbers — they are all in §6. The three structural changes that stuck:

  1. Difference-in-differences (v5–v6). Compare a client to its own recent history, then to its frozen peer group. This survives the permanent 2024 data floor and is computable in production every month.
  2. Calendar time (v7). Everything before v7 measured statistics inside an event-time cell. A queue is a rank within a calendar month over the whole live book. The two answer different questions and only the second can be handed to a sales team.
  3. Ship on precision at capacity, not AUC (v8–v8b). The two metrics disagreed hard: the widest specification had the best AUC and the worst precision. A queue lives in the extreme tail of the ranking; AUC is a global average.

One correction that runs through the whole document

An earlier version of this work reported counterparty coverage as blocked on the PAYS_CPTY and CptyFinEntity ingestion milestones. Those milestones belong to the Neo4j payment graph. This analysis does not use the graph. It reads dsihd01p_dsi.neo4j_payments directly, and that table already carries the counterparty name, the counterparty account and the counterparty's bank on both directions at transaction-day grain. Nothing in the feature roadmap is waiting on an ingestion.
