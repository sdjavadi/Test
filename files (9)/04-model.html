4 · The model and the queue

Calendar-time risk set, rolling-origin hazard, eight specifications, and the capacity-constrained list
Everything before v7 measured statistics inside an event-time cell — how different an attriter looks six months before it leaves, against stayers handed a pseudo-event. That answers a scientific question. A queue is a rank within a calendar month over the whole live book, and the two cannot be converted into each other. This section is built entirely in calendar time.

4.1 Design

ElementChoice
UnitOne row per (client, calendar month) while live and pre-event — 2,005,315 at-risk client-months
Model classDiscrete-time hazard, logistic, ridge-penalised, intercept free. Pure-numpy IRLS — no cluster dependency and a 50×50 normal equation is free
ValidationRolling origin. For test month T and horizon H, training uses only rows with t ≤ T − H, so every training label has already resolved by T. 7 folds, m_idx 24307–24313
Class imbalanceNegatives sampled at 15% in training only, with a King & Zeng intercept correction of ln(0.15). Evaluation runs on the full book, so precision is exact and needs no reweighting
Missing valueslog-dd imputed at 0 (moving exactly with peers) paired with an explicit missingness indicator. A rule leaves the row unscoreable; a model scores it and knows that it did
Data volume313,811 training rows, 541,128 test rows

These two panels must be read together. R_fin_out_n reaches AUC 0.714 at the six-month horizon on the 42% of the book it can score. M4 reaches 0.795 on all of it. An AUC on a third of the book is not the same object as an AUC on the whole book.
The subset is easier for everyone — every model scores higher on it. The point is the ordering: on the rows where fin_out_n is defined, M4 reaches 0.819 against its 0.713. So the fitted model buys both reach and sharper ranking, not just reach. R_fin_out_n's two bars are identical by construction — non-finite scores are dropped from AUC either way.

§4.2 of the handoff, answered

v6 concluded that combining the twelve signals never beats the best single one. That is a true result about an unweighted count of twelve equally-weighted votes. In calendar time even that crude count beats the champion single feature at every horizon (0.748 against 0.714 at six months) and every list length (0.373 against 0.291 at K=250). A properly weighted model beats it again by a wide margin. The v6 headline should be withdrawn, not amended.

4.3 The queue — pricing the trade

This is the trade nobody had priced. M4 dominates every rule at every operating point. The incumbent rule, R_p30, sits along the bottom — at any list length it delivers roughly a tenth of the precision.
At 50 alerts a month M4 runs at 16.5× the base rate. The lift decays as the list lengthens, which is the entire content of a capacity decision.

The like-for-like sentence

For the same 12,667 alerts a month the team already works, the model reaches 5,860 departing clients against 4,103 — 43% more — with 2,041 fewer conversations, 4.8 against 7.4 conversations per client saved, and the same 4-month median warning. There is no axis on which the incumbent wins.

The caveat that belongs next to the precision number

The concentrated list has the highest precision and the shortest warning — a median of two months at K=250 against four at K=12,667. The top of the ranking is dominated by clients already visibly winding down. If the objective is saving relationships rather than recording them accurately, the longer list is the better instrument and the short list is a supplement.
A client in the top 250 for six months running is one relationship conversation, not six. Collapsing re-flags inside three months cuts the model's raw alerts by 36% at K=250 and the incumbent's by 66%. Every alerts-per-true-positive figure quoted in this programme before v7 was the first number presented as if it were the second.

Two tiers, or one list taken deeper

At the same 8,750 alerts, one ranked list taken 1,250 deep beats the two-tier design on precision (0.523 against 0.492) and on recall (0.167 against 0.157). The two-tier proposal should be dropped — it is complexity with a maintenance surface and no payoff.
They find almost entirely different clients, which is the intuitive case for running both. It still does not pay, because the fitted model already absorbs whatever the second list was contributing.

4.4 Validation on the second definition

Base rates differ by only 0.86× (0.91% against 0.78%), so read the ratio. Balance-based scoring keeps 12% of its precision; the payment-based signals keep 81%. That is not degradation, it is a collapse.

What this implies about the primary result

bal_live sums over live legs. A client closing nine of ten accounts at month t shows a 90% balance drop at t and fires A_full_exit at t+2. That is observable at t and it is not leakage — but it means the balance signal on label A is substantially a closure-in-progress detector, not a money-leaving detector. Which is exactly why it evaporates on label B, where nothing closes.

Stated plainly for the handoff: on clients who close, the balance largely records the exit as it happens; on clients who drain, only payments see anything at all. That is the strongest form of this programme's thesis, and it comes from the definition that was being carried as a robustness check.
The incumbent flags 16–19% of the live book every month. The first six months are zero because the rule needs 12 months of history. fin_out_n coverage drifts up from 39% to 46% over the panel and never approaches bal_live's 81–87%.
