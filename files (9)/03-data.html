3 · The data, and what the institution field really is

Sources, inventory, column-level coverage, and the rail × institution analysis
The headline signal of this programme, fin_out_n, is built on cpty_fin_entity_name in the outbound direction — the sparsest field in the payment table at 37%. This section establishes why it is sparse, and the answer is not a data-quality gap. It is arithmetic: the field is populated on ACH, RTP-PRT and wire, and never on card, cheque, PCARD or RTP-P2P.

3.5 Rail × institution — the gating question

A wire carries a beneficiary bank. A cheque carries an image and a payee line. A debit-card signature transaction has an acquirer, not a receiving institution. If cpty_fin_entity_name is populated as a function of the rail, then fin_out_n is not a bank count — it is a rail-activity count, and its coverage ceiling is not fixable.

It is 1.0000 or 0.0000 for every rail. Not sparse — deterministic. ACH, RTP-PRT and WIRE carry the institution; DEBIT_CARD_SIGNATURE, CHECK, PCARD and RTP-P2P never do. The one exception is inbound CHECK, which carries the institution and the account but never the name (0.0000) — the paying bank is on the item, the payer's name is not parsed.
ACH 0.3584 × 1.000, plus RTP-PRT 0.0102 × 0.996, plus WIRE 0.0012 × 1.000 = 0.3698, against a measured overall outbound has_fin of 0.3700. The 63 points that are missing are 59 points of debit card plus 4 points of cheque, PCARD and RTP-P2P. No field choice moves this.
96.9% is ACH. Wire is 0.3%. fin_out_n should be restated everywhere as the number of distinct institutions a client sends ACH to. It is not a wire signal — an earlier hypothesis to that effect is refuted below.
This is the nuance that changes how the 37% should be read. Debit card is 59.1% of outbound transactions and 1.1% of outbound dollars. Wire is 0.1% of transactions and 56.8% of dollars. Weighted by money rather than transaction count, institution coverage is 96.1%, not 37%. The coverage problem is a retail-transaction-count problem; for a Treasury Management book measured in dollars, the field is nearly complete.

Is it a wire proxy? No — and it is better where wires are absent

Sending a wire raises the chance of having an institution count from 66.1% to 91.2% — an effect, but nowhere near determinism. Two thirds of client-months with no wire at all still carry a fin_out_n, because they send ACH.
The decisive test. fin_out_n performs better among non-wire clients (0.7430) than among wire users (0.6763), where bal_live beats it outright. It is not a wire detector. What it is scarce for is clients whose outbound activity is card and cheque — and those are exactly the clients where it cannot be computed at all (32% scoreable).
84,707 client-months are cheque-only and 13,774 are card-only. None of them can ever be scored by an institution feature. That is 98,481 client-months, roughly 5% of the panel, structurally invisible to the signal this programme has been briefing.

3.6 Counterparty keys — measured before switching

The obvious response to a 39.5%-covered account id sitting beside a 99.9%-covered name is to re-key on the name. That is a change to what a counterparty is, so it was measured first. Two failure modes, opposite in direction and both silent.

Under-merge is mild outbound (3.6% of real entities split into two or more) and material inbound (10.8%). Over-merge is the dangerous one: one normalised outbound name covers up to 3,264 distinct accounts, and inbound up to 190,464.
Built on a median-multiple rule (15× the median, floored at 500) rather than a percentile, because a percentile flags a fixed fraction by construction. It catches 21,773 names — 0.06% of 38,808,093 — carrying 40.7% of all customer-counterparty links. That is the right shape: a tiny number of processor and biller strings carrying an enormous share of the edges.

Two problems with the registry as built

It was fitted on the whole bank, not the study population. The worst over-merge offenders outbound are MOM (1,146 accounts), DAD (599), JOSE RODRIGUEZ (898), CHECKING (667), LINK (574) — retail peer-to-peer strings that a Treasury Management client would never pay. The threshold is calibrated to consumer volumes.

It strips 40.7% of links. For a corporate client, AMEX EPAYMENT or CAPITAL ONE may be a genuine, material counterparty relationship. Excluding them is defensible for churn measurement and lossy for everything else — and it is why the name-keyed features ended up with lower coverage (0.464) than the account-keyed ones (0.504).
A name key that treats every payment labelled MOM as the same counterparty is not measuring a business relationship. This is the concrete reason the re-keying recommendation was withdrawn.
