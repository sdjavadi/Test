1 · What we are predicting

Attrition definitions, closure mechanics, the dependent variable, and the population at risk
"A client left" is not one event. A client can close every account, or keep them open and move the money out, or simply shrink. Those are three different populations with three different signatures, and conflating them is what made several earlier runs unreadable.

1.1 The objective

Two questions, stated at the start of the programme:

  1. Does payment behaviour flag a departing treasury client earlier than the deposit balance does? — answered: yes, and more importantly it flags clients the balance never sees.
  2. Can it separate moving to a competitor from business contraction? — still open. A first attempt returned a clean null; see §6.

1.2 Three definitions of attrition

Definition Rule Raw events Qualified Monthly hazard Role
A_full_exit Every account non-live, and it stays that way 20248 16384 0.9115% Primary label
B_bal_exit Balance < 5% of trailing-12 median, held 3 months 14016 14016 0.7798% Validation label
C_p30 3-month avg < 0.70 x prior-6 avg — the incumbent rule 57551 57551 — Incumbent benchmark
Qualification requires 12 months of history and 6 live months before the event, so a client already winding down at panel start is not counted as an exit. It removes 3,864 of A's raw events and none of B's or C's. B is a strict subset of C — 14,016 = 14,016.

Why A_full_exit is primary

It is the only definition that is unambiguously a departure rather than a decline. It is absorbing — of 65,975 accounts that ever went fully non-live, only 314 (0.5%) came back — and it agrees with the closed_dt field on 64,102 accounts at a one-month median gap. B_bal_exit is carried as a validation label throughout because it is the harder and more commercially painful population: the client keeps the relationship open and takes the money elsewhere.

1.3 The rule in production today

The incumbent is a 30% balance rule: flag a client whose three-month average balance falls below 0.70 of its prior-six-month average. Measured against A_full_exit's 16,384 qualified attriters:

It also fires on 43,143 customers who never left. Median warning where it does fire usefully is 2.0 months (p25 0, p75 7). 36% of attriters get no usable warning at all.

1.4 Closure mechanics — the correction that drives everything

acct_status carries two code systems in one column: numeric codes from HOGAN and single letters from the sweep source. The first run of this programme was voided because it used 'C' (a sweep code, 3,182 accounts) as the closure marker instead of 07 and 08 (112,107 accounts).

08 PURGEABLE is post-07 retention, not a distinct reason — mean 264.7 days since closed_dt against 98.9 for 07, both at share_zero_bal = 1.000. Sweep C is a leg state, not an account state: only 76 accounts are ever judged non-live purely because sweep legs are idle.

Grain

acct_full_acct_id + sub_product_cd + edw_tda_load_dt. Zero duplicate keys at that grain, against 3,675,351 duplicates at account-day. Sweep legs are not duplicates — account balance is the sum over legs. A closure label is the first month after the last month with a live leg.

1.5 The dependent variable

For a client c at calendar month t and horizon H:

y_H(c, t) = 1  if the event month for c falls in (t, t + H]
           = 0  otherwise
           = undefined  if t + H is outside the panel and no event occurred

Primary horizon H = 6 months; H = 1 and H = 3 are reported alongside. Three rules make this honest:

  • Risk set. A row exists only where the client has at least one live account at t and has not yet had the event. 2,005,315 at-risk client-months.
  • Observability. A row whose panel ends before t + H and which has no event is dropped, not zeroed. Zeroing it would quietly relabel every right-censored client a stayer.
  • Nothing at or after t enters a feature. The difference-in-differences transform uses t and the window t−6 … t−4; the peer median is cross-sectional within the same calendar month, which is available in production at t; the frozen decile comes from the client's first three observed months.

Payments is the whole bank: 12.8 billion transactions and 12.4M mdm ids against a deposit book of 229,363 accounts. Real coverage is 72% of deposit accounts and 80% of deposit customers payment-visible. Everything in this report is computed on the 94,179 clients visible in both.

The book shrinks from 79,135 to 69,038 over the panel — that is the risk set contracting as clients exit and are removed, not the bank losing 13% of its deposit customers. m_idx is an absolute month index (~24289–24319), not a 0-based panel position; treating it as 0-based silently produced 24,296 empty evaluation folds in one run.
